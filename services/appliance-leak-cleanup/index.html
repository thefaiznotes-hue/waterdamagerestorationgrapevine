Dishwasher, washing-machine, refrigerator, and water-heater leaks can wet cabinets and flooring before they become obvious. We assess nearby materials and plan cleanup based on the source and extent of moisture.

BEFORE CLEANUP BEGINS

Your first practical step.

Shut off the appliance water supply if safe. Avoid touching electrical plugs or controls in a wet area. Arrange the appliance or plumbing repair and tell us whether the leak appears recent or ongoing.

If there is an immediate threat to life or safety, call 911.

YOUR PROPERTY, YOUR PLAN

A Grapevine assessment starts with details.

When you call, share your Grapevine address, property type, affected rooms, and how long the materials may have been wet. Access, flooring, wall construction, and the water source all affect the cleanup plan.

What affects the scope?

The amount of water is only part of the picture. Moisture beneath flooring, inside cabinets, or behind walls can change what needs drying or removal. Ask about the proposed work before it starts.

QUESTIONS, ANSWERED

Know what comes next.

Can a small leak damage cabinets?

Yes. Water can reach cabinet bases, adjoining walls, and flooring seams. The visible puddle does not always show the full affected area.

What information should I have ready?

Your address, water source if known, the affected rooms, and any access or electrical concerns. Photos can help explain the situation, but a full scope may need an on-site assessment.
